**Explanation of the discussion in the Talmud of the verse, My soul shall cry in the hidden parts.**

*This is a note the Baal HaLeshem added to the text of this chapter discussing two versions of the Talmudic text. The girsa (version) found in our editions of the Talmud follows Rashi, according to which joy is found in the outer chambers, but weeping in the inner chambers. Rabbi Elyashiv prefers the girsa of Rabbeinu Chananel – that weeping is in the outer chambers, and joy in the inner chambers, and he makes the case as to why this version is the primary one.*

**The above text** that weeping is in the outer chambers, and joy in the inner chambers **is found in an older version** of the Talmud, favored by Rabbeinu Chananel. **However, the version in the current editions of the Talmud follow Rashi's *girsa*, which is the opposite of the one stated above. This is the wording of the Talmud as currently printed: The verse** (Jeremiah 13:17) **states:** **"But if you will not hear it, My soul shall weep in secret** \[*b'mistarim*\] **for your grandeur."** **Rav Shmuel bar Inya said in the name of Rav: The blessed Holy One has a place** where He weeps, **and its name is *Mistarim*. What** is the meaning of **"for your grandeur"? Rav Shmuel bar Yitzchak said:** G-d weeps **for the grandeur of the Jewish people which was taken from them and given to** the gentile **nations. Rav Shmuel bar Nachmani said:** He weeps **for the grandeur of the Kingdom of Heaven,** which was withdrawn from the world.

The Gemara asks: **But is there weeping before the blessed Holy One? Didn't Rav Pappa say: There is no sadness before the blessed Holy One as the verse states** (I Chronicles 16:27)**: "Grandeur and majesty are before Him; strength and gladness are in His place"**? The Gemara responds: **This is** **not a difficulty. This** statement that Gd weeps is referring to **the innermost chambers,** where He can weep in secret, whereas **the other** statement, that He does not weep, is referring to **the outer chambers.**

The Gemara then asks: **And is there none** no weeping **in the outer chambers? Doesn't the verse state** (Isaiah 22:12)**: "And the Lord, G-d of legions, called it a day for weeping and mourning, and shaving off their hair, and donning sackcloth"**? The Gemara responds: **The destruction of the Beit HaMikdash** the Temple **is different, as even the Angels of Peace cried, as the verse** (Isaiah 33:7) **states: "Behold, the valiant ones weep outside; the Angels of Peace weep bitterly."** This is the end of the quote from the Gemara.

**Rashi explains:** The statement that there is weeping refers to **in the innermost chambers, since the verse states, *b'mistarim*** in secret, i.e. the inner chambers. And he adds: **This is the accepted version: And is there none** no weeping **in the outer chambers? Doesn't the verse state, "He called** it a day for weeping and mourning"**? But wherever the word 'called' ויקרא** appears **it signifies audible sound. This is as far as Rashi explains.**

**Now this** Rashi's **version is cited in a number of works written after Rashi's time,** e.g. ***Sefer Ha-Ikkarim,* 4:23; *Sefer Ha-Akeida Sha'ar* 87; and so too in *Yalkut Yirmeya*** on the verse **13:17. The *Yalkut* was also compiled after Rashi's time, as is known from the works of historians – see** e.g. ***Seder HaDorot,*** **end of section 1\.**

**However, from Rashi's** own **words – 'this is the accepted version' – it is evident that he also had another version in front of him. And this is** indeed **noted by the Radvaz in *Magen Avot* \#2 that the version of older works** it states the opposite: **This** statement that G-d weeps is referring to **the *outer* chambers,** whereas **the other** statement, where there is no weeping, refers to **the *inner* chambers.** And when the Gemara asks: **And is there no weeping…** the older version continues: **in the *inner* chambers? Doesn't the verse state**: "And on that day the Lord **called**…"

**The older version is also cited in Rabbi Yosef Gikatilia's *Sha'arei Tzedek*** in the section on the ***sefirah*** **of *binah.* And similarly in Rabbi Todros HaLevi's *Otzar HaKavod,* Tractate *Chagiga*** 5b: **'There are other works where it states:** "And is there no weeping **in the outer chambers?" (i.e. Rashi's version) but these cannot be correct. See there (**although **there are a few words missing there).**

**But now we have the benefit of Rabbi Chananel's work which was printed in the new Vilna edition of the Talmud from the manuscript. And he too writes exclusively according to the old version, just like the Radvaz.**

**According to Rashi's version then the Gemara's opinion was initially that "in the inner chambers there is weeping, but not in the outer chambers." But the older version states the opposite, that "in the *outer* chambers there is weeping, but not in the *inner* chambers." But according to the conclusion, when the Beit HaMikdash was destroyed there was weeping everywhere** in both the inner and outer chambers. **Here there is no disagreement among the versions. But leaving aside the destruction of the Beit HaMikdash, there is a distinction between the versions, from one point of view to its opposite.**

**What motivated Rashi to opt for the other version is straightforward – because the term 'in secret**' \[*b'mistarim*\] **clearly implies only the inner chamber** which is concealed from the public. **But see Rabbeinu Chananel's explanation of why** *mistarim* **refers to the outer chambers, whereas in the inner chambers there is no weeping, as per the older version. However, this matter needs some 'spice'** added to it to make it more palatable.

**The concept of the inner chambers and outer chambers is discussed in the Zohar** II, ***Shemot* 18a. The inner chambers are the sanctuary of Atzilut, whereas the outer chambers are the sanctuary of the worlds of BiY"A. The Zohar ibid. explains that with the destruction of the Beit HaMikdash there was weeping in the inner chambers as well, but aside from that destruction, there is no sadness in the presence of the blessed Holy One –** i.e. **only in the outer chambers but not in the inner ones, as is explained in Zohar** III, ***Vayikra*** **15b. And this is like the view of Rabbeinu Chananel and the older version.**

**But there is another concept regarding the inner and outer chambers, and both of these are in Atzilut itself, for the letters י"ה** *yud, hei* **of the Name are** **the inner chambers, meaning the sanctuaries of Abba and Imma. But the letters ו"ה** *vav, hei* **of the Name are the outer chambers, i.e. the sanctuaries of ze'ir anpin and nukvah** (*malchut*), **since ze'ir anpin and nukvah are below the *parsa*** the dividing line between ***Arich Anpin*** and the other *partzufim* **so that they** ze'ir anpin and nukvah **are Arich's *netzach, hod, yesod***[^1] **– he** ze'ir anpin corresponding to ***netzach*** **and she** *nukvah* corresponding to ***hod.*** **Now all** the three *sefirot **netzach, hod and yesod*** **are separate from the body** the main *sefirot,* just as the legs are separate from the torso, **as the *Sifra d'Tzniuta*** **beginning of chapter 2 states. See the GRA ad loc.**

**Abba and Imma are also called *nistarot*** 'concealed aspects' **as Eliyahu taught**[^2]**:** "the *nistarot* – referring to *chochmah* and *binah* – are for G-d alone." **And therefore they are the 'inner chambers,'** **whereas ze'ir anpin and nukvah are the 'outer chambers.' So stated the Rabbi** Rabbi Yitzchak Luria, the Arizal, in *Eitz Chaim Sha'ar **Pnimiut v'Chitzoniyut, drush*** **9: Abba and Imma are the 'inner chambers,' referring to the sanctuaries of Abba and Imma, as mentioned.**

**Thus we have explained that the 'inner chambers' and the 'outer chambers' represent two concepts: 1\) The 'inner chambers' are Atzilut, and the 'outer chambers' are BiY"A; 2\) In Atzilut itself Abba and Imma are the 'inner chambers,' and *ze'ir anpin* and *nukvah* are the 'outer chambers.'**

We can now explain the statement of the Rabbis: **'The blessed Holy One has a place** **and its name is *Mistarim*' in a similar manner** to our explanation above. **Here too, there are two aspects: 1\) the chamber of the Holy of Holies of Beriah where *malchut* of Atzilut is vested – this is the mystery of** 'the appearance of **a Man sitting upon the Throne'** (see Ezekiel 1:26). **And she** *malchut* of Atzilut **is called 'Esther' because she is concealed within the 'head'** i.e. Abba and Imma / *chochmah* and *binah* **of Beriah. This is because** the name **Esther is an expression of concealment,**[^3] **as *Raya Mehemna* in** Zohar III ***Teitzei*** **76a states. All of this is explain in the words of the Arizal, *Sha'ar Kisei haKavod*. See chapters 2, 5, 6 there.**

**This is the meaning of the statement 'The blessed Holy One has a place** **and its name is *Mistarim*.' And this is what Rabbeinu Chananel said – that the place called '*Mistarim*' refers to the outer chambers, since it is the Holy of Holies of the World of Beriah, which is an external aspect** compared with Atzilut, **as mentioned. It is only there that the *Shechinah*** **is called Esther because of her** state of **concealment there, since Atzilut is her primary place, as** the rabbis **established** in the *Aleinu* prayer: **"The abode of His majesty is in the loftiest heights," for there she is in full revelation, and she is the final *hei* of** the Name **Havaye. But when she descends into BiY"A she becomes concealed, and** the Name **Havaye becomes clothed within** the Name **A-d-o-n-ai.**

**Similarly, Beriah is called by the Name E-lohim, which has the same *gematria*** numerical value **as הכס"א – the Throne** (=86), **since the World of Beriah is the Realm of the Throne, as is known. But in the World of Atzilut she is** part of **the Name Havaye itself, the final *hei* thereof, for that is where she is fully revealed, as mentioned.**

**Now at the beginning of the discussion, the Gemara resolves the apparent contradiction** between two statements by explaining that **'This** statement refers to **the innermost chambers,** whereas **the other** statement, refers to **the outer chambers' and that in the inner chamber there is no weeping, and weeping is only in the outer chambers. The questioner (or the Gemara itself, presenting contrasting viewpoints which were not actually expressed) is of the opinion that when the Beit HaMikdash was destroyed there was weeping only in the outer chamber, and the fact that the verse states, "My soul shall weep in secret** \[*b'mistarim*\]**"** **refers to the place called '*Mistarim*' which is the chamber of the Holy of Holies of Beriah, where the Shechinah is clothed and becomes concealed. And that is where the weeping takes place, since it is always the outer chamber** when compared with Atzilut. **And therefore the question arises "and in the inner chambers is there no weeping? Doesn't the verse state: "And on that day A-d-o-n-ai, Havaye Tzva'ot called it a day for weeping and mourning…"**?

**Now the verse here uses three holy Names** A-d-o-n-ai, Havaye, and Tzva'ot – in that order – **and they encompass both aspects of the 'inner chambers' we spoke about above – the Name A-d-o-n-ai is in *malchut* of Atzilut, as the Zohar** III, ***Vayikra*** **11a-b states, and in several other places.**

**\[But the fact that we stated above that this** Name **is in Beriah, the intention was that in Beriah even the Name Havaye therein is concealed within the Name A-d-o-n-ai exclusively, so that only the light of the Name A-d-o-n-ai is perceived, and so** although **it is written as י-ה-ו-ה** (Havaye) **it is read as א-ד-נ-י** (A-d-o-n-ai).

**But in Atzilut, where the Name Havaye is primary, the Name A-d-o-n-ai is** only **secondary, since its presence there is merely as the root of the worlds of BiY"A, and it** constitutes **the outer aspect thereof. However, the essence** of Atzilut **is the Name Havaye exclusively** i.e. **Havaye fully iterated with the letter *hei*** as the *milui* ***–*** יו"ד ה"ה ו"ו ה"ה **which is the Name Ba"n** \=52, that numerically **\= 2x Havaye**[^4] (2x26=52) **since it encompasses all of Atzilut, and it is the final *hei* of the macro Name Havaye, because** as explained earlier **there are macro and micro** revelations of the Name Havaye, **each of which is illuminated in its own way by the light of the entire Name Havaye. And this is besides the macro Name Havaye that encompasses the entire Atzilut with the light of a single** instance of the Name **Havaye. In this respect, *malchut* is merely one letter thereof, i.e. the final *hei*** of this Name Havaye.

**This is also found in Midrash *Pesikta Rabbati* 21: The Name of the Holy One, blessed is He, is Tzva'ot** (usually translated as legions)**. Rabbi Yehuda bar Simon said: 'The bottom line is that this does not apply only to the entire Name, but even a single letter of His Name creates a legion, just like His entire Name.'**

**This is just as we said – that every letter of the blessed Name Havaye includes the entire Name, including the final *hei*, which is the Name Havaye in *malchut* of Atzilut – the very essence and primary aspect of Atzilut within her.**

**But since she** *malchut* **is also the root of the Worlds of BiY"A, the revelation of the Name A-d-o-n-ai (and all the other Names) is** also **manifested in her, i.e. in her outer aspects.**

**Thus, in any event, the fact is that the Name A-d-o-n-ai is in *malchut* of Atzilut, which is the Name A-d-o-n-ai in the verse we are discussing.\]**

Now **the Name Havaye Tzva'ot is in *nh"y*** *netzach, hod, yesod* **of Atzilut (as it states in Tikunim, Tikun 18, p. 34a – Havaye Tzva'ot is the covenant of circumcision**  i.e. *yesod* **and the two thighs** i.e. supports **of truth** – *netzach* and *hod*). **And they also include *chesed, gevurah, tiferet* of Atzilut, as the Rabbi** the Arizal **states in the *kavanot* of *Kedushah*. See the Intro to the Tikunim p. 11b, and the GRA there beginning with the word אינון and Zohar** III **p. 11b.**

**Thus, included in the Name A-d-o-n-ai Havaye Tzva'ot are all the seven lower *sefirot* of Atzilut. These are the inner chambers of the first aspect we mentioned above** i.e. where we explained that the 'inner chambers' and the 'outer chambers' can be explained in two ways, the first of which is that the 'inner chambers' are Atzilut, and the 'outer chambers' are BiY"A.

**Now the verse** cited above (Isaiah 22:12) **is written with the Name Havaye with the vowels of *Elokim*** וַיִּקְרָא אֲדֹנָ"י **יְהוִ"ה** צְבָאוֹת  **which is *binah*, as the Zohar** III **10b states, i.e. supernal Imma as she includes the *chochmah* of Abba, as** the Zohar III ***parshat Acharei* 65a states. See the note 7 of Rabbi Chaim Vital on that section. Thus Abba and Imma are both included in the Name Havaye with the vowels of *Elokim* (see Zohar I, 26a:  Havaye Elokim – Abba and Imma). And these are the inner chambers of the second explanation mentioned above** – where Abba and Imma are the 'inner chambers,' and ze'ir anpin and nukvah are the 'outer chambers' *in Atzilut itself*.

**This then is the question the Gemara** **poses** according to the version favored by Rabbeinu Chananel**: And is there no weeping** **in the *inner* chambers? Doesn't the verse state** (Isaiah 22:12)**: "And on that day the Lord, G-d of legions, called it a day for weeping and mourning…"?**

**The Name** **Havaye is written here with the vowels of Elokim,** as explained above, **so that both these Names allude to both aspects of the inner chambers** 1\) where the 'inner chambers' are Atzilut, and the 'outer chambers' are BiY"A**;** 2\) where Abba and Imma are the 'inner chambers,' and ze'ir anpin and nukvah are the 'outer chambers' *in Atzilut itself*. **Accordingly,** it would seem that **there is weeping in the 'inner chambers' according to both explanations\! But the Gemara responds: The destruction of the Beit HaMikdash is different, as even the Angels of Peace wept, as the verse states: "Behold, the valiant ones weep outside; the Angels of Peace…"**

**At first glance this is surprising. After the Gemara proves that there was weeping in the inner chambers as well, at the very highest levels** i.e. even in Abba and Imma of Atzilut, **what is the meaning of** **"*even*** the Angels of Peace cried"? If there was weeping even in the inner chambers, how much more so in the outer chambers? **And what does** the fact that they were **Angels of Peace** **add?**

**However, the idea is this: As is known, the angels do not have free choice, and they are fixed and unchanging in all their attributes, similar to fire and water where fire is dry and water is wet and they cannot change and be transformed from one into the other. The same is true of the angels – one that is appointed over peace is the vessel for all sorts of goodness and blessing, and cannot be transformed into weeping and moaning and groaning. But at the time of the destruction of the Beit HaMikdash, may heaven have pity, the orders of Creation above and below were disrupted, as our Sages expound on the verse** (Lamentations 2:2)**, "He profaned the kingdom and its leaders," cited in Rashi** ad loc. **'the angels above were exchanged.' And this is the meaning of the statement that "the destruction of the Beit HaMikdash is different," for the entire order of Creation was upended. And therefore it is not surprising that there was weeping in even the inner chambers above to the loftiest levels.**

**Similarly, the *Midrash Tehillim* 20 states that when the Beit HaMikdash was destroyed weeping and wailing overtook the entire world, as the verse states: "And A-d-o-n-ai, Havaye Tzva'ot called it a day for weeping and mourning…" Then the ministering angels said before Him: "Are these** weeping and mourning **indeed before You? Is it not written, "Grandeur and majesty are before Him; strength and gladness are in His place"? To which He replied: "Is My house not destroyed and weren't My children led away in chains, that I shouldn't be distraught? Is it not written** (Psalms 91:15), **'I am with him in** times of **distress.'" See there.**

**So it turns out that there was weeping in both aspects of the inner chambers, to the very loftiest levels. And how much more so was there weeping down below, as *Midrash Tehillim* states, that weeping and wailing overtook the entire world.**

**However, when the verse states, "…My soul shall weep in secret** \[*b'Mistarim*\] **for your grandeur," and our Sages explain that 'The blessed Holy One has a place** where He weeps, **and its name is *Mistarim*' we cannot explain as we did above – that this refers to the Holy of Holies of Beriah – because according to the Gemara's conclusion there was weeping even in the inner chambers of Atzilut, as mentioned.**

**But there is another aspect of the place** called ***Mistarim*** **which is at the loftiest of levels – the inner chambers of this highest aspect, referred to earlier as the letters י"ה** (*yud, hei*) **of the Name i.e. Abba and Imma. At those levels too there is a place called *Mistarim*, regarding which the verse states, "My soul shall weep in secret** \[*b'mistarim*\] **for your grandeur," as the Arizal explains in *Sha'ar Ruach HaKodesh* regarding the rectification of conceit**, the negative side of grandeur, **and similarly in *Sefer HaYichudim*** **– that the sin of conceit** even **has an impact on the letters י"ה** *yud, hei* **of the Name, since the letters י"ה** *yud, hei* **have the same *gematria*** numerical value **as** the word for **conceit גאו"ה.**

**However, the outside forces** the *chitzonim*[^5] **do not impact the letters י"ה themselves, only their *milui*** (iteration). **Now there are three** possible ***miluim* of the letters י"ה – with a *yud*, like this: יו"ד ה"י; or with an *aleph* יו"ד ה"א or with a *hei* יו"ד ה"ה. The blemish cause by conceit impacts only the latter** sequence **with a *milui hei***, **and only the letters of the *milui* itself: ו'ד'ה which has the same letters as דָּוָה** – **anguish**[^6] **– which is also the *gematria* of  גאו"ה**(conceit).

**Now the *milui* letters are called '*mistarim'* because their iteration is hidden and concealed within the primary letters. So this then is the meaning of "…My soul shall weep *b'mistarim* for your grandeur (גוה)."**

\[Note that **the word גוה here is missing an *aleph*** – it should read ג**א**וה \= grandeur – **because of the blemish done to it** to His grandeur. **For גאוה** grandeur **is appropriate only for the blessed Holy One, as the verse** (Psalms 93:1) **states:** ה' מָלָךְ **גֵּאוּת** לָבֵשׁ **– "G-d has donned grandeur." Now the word גֵּאוּת is written in the plural – once for the primary letters, and once for the *milui*, as mentioned, both of which are appropriate only for the blessed Holy One alone.**

**Thus, when there is a blemish in the *milui*, the verse states, "…My soul shall weep *b'mistarim* for your grandeur (גוה)" – meaning to say, due to the blemish made in it. And this is why the word is written with** only **a *vav*** without the *aleph,* **because the grandeur of the Kingdom of Heaven has been withdrawn, may He have compassion for us,** and when His grandeur **descends below, it does not descend the way it is Above, Heaven forfend, Heaven forfend, and therefore גוה is written in the defective form,** without the *aleph.*\]

**Now the three *miluim* of the letter *hei*, which are ה"י and ה"א and ה"ה** together **have the *gematria* of 31 \= the Name  א-לthat appears in the verse** (Exodus 34:6-7) **of the Thirteen Attributes of Divine Mercy א-ל רחום וחנון etc.**

**The Name א-ל** **is** also **the first** **of the 13 *Tikunei Dikna,***[^7] situated **below the *pe'ot*** sidelocks **of the head, at the top of the beard, next to the ears. (There lies the secret of "From the straits I called out to י"ה"** (Psalms 118:5) **– the name י"ה we mentioned earlier. At the top** of the beard the *pe'ot* **are narrower, and below, near the mouth, they broaden).** **Thus they are** actually **two Names, one to the right and one to the left** corresponding to the two *pe'ot*, **and each of them comprises three aspects** of the Name א-ל  **– א-ל שד"י and  א-ל הוי"הand א-ל אדנ"י, making six times** that **the Name א-ל** is manifested here **and all of them emerge from the Name י-ה with its *milui*.**

**Now six times the Name א-ל is the numerical value** *gematria* **of מקו"ם** place \= 186, **which is the mystical secret of** the statement of the Sages cited earlier: **'The blessed Holy One has a place,** **and its name is *Mistarim,'* the actuality of which is six times the Name א-ל**, **which is the** *gematria* **of מקו"ם, fashioned out of the letter** **ה in its three iterations,** as mentioned above, **of the *milui* of the final *hei* exclusively*.* But the blemish is only in the *milui*, which is the *mistarim*,** as we explained, **and only in the** ***milui*** **of the** final ***hei*, which is the final *milui,* comprising the letters *dalet, vav, hei*** **דוה which has the same *germatria* as** **grandeur (גאוה).**

**This then is the meaning of the verse, "…My soul shall weep *b'mistarim* for your grandeur (גוה)" – because of the grandeur of the Kingdom of Heaven that was withdrawn at the time of the destruction** of the Beit HaMikdash**, may the Merciful One have compassion for us.** **The destruction of the First Beit HaMikdash impacted the first *hei* of the blessed Name Havaye, as *Zohar II,* 9b states, and look into** ibid. **4a. See also *Zohar I*, 26a. Now the first *hei*** of the blessed Name Havaye **is always united with the *yud* (since they are two beloved friends who do not part**[^8]**) and that is the place of the grandeur of the Kingdom of Heaven, as the verse** (Psalms 93:1) **states: ה' מָלָךְ גֵּאוּת לָבֵשׁ – "G-d has donned grandeur," as mentioned above.**

**Given all of this** analysis above **it is clear that the place of *mistarim* is in the inner chambers of Atzilut as well.**

**Accordingly** we must conclude that **there are two aspects: One** aspect of ***mistarim*** **is located in the outer chambers, and this is in the Holy of Holies of the World of Beriah, as explained above; and the other** aspect of *mistarim* **is in the inner chambers of Atzilut, in the *milui* of the letters י"ה. The destruction** of the Beit HaMikdash **caused weeping in both aspects, and this is why the verse states *mistarim* in the plural** – as the \-*im* suffix at the end of the word indicates.

**Now regarding these two aspects of weeping, which took place in the two localities of *mistarim*, the Sages in** Tractate ***Chagiga*** **5b asked: "What** is the meaning of **"for your grandeur"? Rav Shmuel bar Yitzchak said:** G-d weeps **for the grandeur of the Jewish people which was taken from them and given….** to the gentile nations**. Rav Shmuel bar Nachmani said:** He weeps **for the grandeur of the Kingdom of Heaven,** which was withdrawn from the world.

According to the above analysis, these are not contradictory views. **Rav Shmuel bar Yitzchak is referring to the weeping in the outer chambers, for that is where the grandeur of Israel is located. This is because the secret of the Shechinah,** as manifested in **in the Chamber of the Holy of Holies of the World of Beriah, descended into the Chamber of the Holy of Holies of the Beit HaMikdash down below when the Beit HaMikdash stood, for** the Chamber of the Holy of Holies below **corresponds to the Chamber of the Holy of Holies of the World of Beriah, as the Arizal writes** **in *Drushei Ma'n uMa'd*, end of *drush* 1\. And it** the Holy of Holies of Beriah **is the Beit HaMikdash on high, regarding which our Sages state in** Tractate ***Chagiga*** **12b: "The Archangel Michael stands and makes sacrificial offerings there of the souls righteous Tsaddikim before the Holy One, blessed is He." The *Midrash haNe'elam* in *Zohar Chadash, Noach* (beginning with the words הפתח השלישי)** **explains this. (**The *Zohar Chadash* **states there that this is not in the same manner as all other sacrificial offerings, but rather like a person presenting a gift to a king. So too, the Archangel Michael proffers the souls of righteous Tsaddikim before the Divine Presence** the Shechinah **in the Supernal Beit HaMikdash, which is the Chamber of the Holy of Holies of the World of Beriah).**

**When the Beit HaMikdash stood, then the Supernal Beit HaMikdash descended into the Beit HaMikdash below, similar to the statement in** Tractate ***Sukkah*** **5a: "The Throne extended itself downward…" In the same manner Atzilut extended down until the Ark and the Cherubim** on top of the Ark, **and the worlds of BiY"A received their life-force and sustenance only via the Beit HaMikdash below. The intention in all of this was to contract** His Presence **and dwell within the Jewish People themselves, as the verse** (Exodus25:8) **states, "Make for Me a Beit HaMikdash, so that I may dwell within them…"** The verse does not say **"within *it*," but "within *them*"** – within each Israelite.

**This was indeed Moses' request** when he asked (Exodus 34:9) "If I have now found favor in your eyes… **let the Lord go now in our midst" – within the Israelites, literally.** **This is such a great and sublime quality that there is nothing loftier than it, as the GRA writes in *Aderet Eliyahu, Ki Tisa***, **and this is the grander of Israel. But with the destruction of the Beit HaMikdash, may the Merciful One spare us, the Shechinah with withdrawn above as it states in** Tractate ***Rosh HaShana*** **31a: "She** the Shechinah **ascended and returned to her place, as the verse** (Hosea 5:15) **states, "I will leave and return to My place."**

**Nowadays the *shefa*** the flow of spiritual sustenance **is drawn down through exceedingly crooked paths** (**see *Zohar*** II ***Terumah,*** **p. 152b**).

**This then is what Rav Shmuel bar Yitzchak was saying –** the weeping is "**for the grandeur of the Jewish people which was taken from them and given….** to the gentiles," **and he is referring to the weeping in the *mistarim* of the outer chambers, for that is where weeping for the grandeur of Israel that has been rescinded takes place.**

**But Rav Shmuel bar Nachmani said:** "He weeps **for the grandeur of the Kingdom of Heaven,** which was withdrawn from the world." **He is referring to the weeping in the *mistarim*** **of the inner chambers in Atzilut, regarding which it states, "G-d has donned grandeur."** This aspect of *mistarim* is in the *milui* of **the letters י"ה** (*yud, hei*) **of the Name, as explained above. When the Beit HaMikdash stood, and the revelation of the *Shechinah* was down below, then the verse "G-d has donned grandeur" was fulfilled, since the grandeur of the Kingdom of Heaven was revealed throughout the earth. But with the destruction** of the Beit HaMikdash, **may the Merciful One spare us,** **it states "…My soul shall weep *b'mistarim* for Your grandeur." Examine the words of the Arizal in** *Eitz Chaim Sha'ar **Mi'ut haYare'ach*** **chaps 2-3.**

As we explained above, **the grandeur of the Kingdom of Heaven is in the letters י"ה of the Name, whereas the grandeur of Israel in the letter ו"ה of the Name, and they depend upon one another, as** Tractate ***Megillah*** **29a and Midrash Tehillim 9:14 and a number of other works state.**[^9]

**This is also the concept of** when **'the Name is complete and the Throne is complete' discussed in *Pesikta* on the verse** (Deut. 25:17) **"Remember** what Amalek did to you" **cited in Rashi at the end of *Beshalach*. Because** when **'the Name is complete' this is the grandeur of the Kingdom of Heaven, and** when **'the Throne is complete' is the grandeur of Israel, as the verse** (I Chronicles 29:23) **states, "and Solomon sat on the Throne of G-d". See Zohar I p. 243a; Tikunei Zohar Chadash 37b and similarly in Tikunim, Tikun 22, p. 76a. This is enough for now.**

**Now all of this is according to the older prior version of the Talmud, elucidated by Rabbeinu Chananel, which is the primary version as the Radvaz** maintains **and Rabbi Todros HaLevi writes in Otzar HaKavod, as explained above.**

**However, we found that one of the holy Sages of Truth** – a designation for master Kabbalists – **a Master of the Secret Teachings, Rabbi Moshe Cordovero in Pardes, *Sha'ar Mehut v'Hanhaga* chap. 12 accepts Rashi's version, that in the inner chambers there is weeping, but not in the outer chambers. This is due to the principle that any outflow is only from above, and whatever is below is what they receive from above. Accordingly, when they said** according to this version **that there is none in the outer chambers, this is because they have nothing of their own, whereas it is in the inner chambers, since that is the source. He also cites a few statements from the Zohar to prove his point.**

**Nevertheless, in my own impoverished view the main thrust of his words is very surprising. For it is very well known that all strict judgments above are all sweetened and fragrant and are the flames of compassion, and this is the entire subject of the statement of our Sages that there is no sadness before the blessed Holy One, but only** as the verse (I Chronicles 16:27) states **"might and joy are in His place". Might and joy are also only from the revelation of *gevurot*** (attributes that generally tend to judgment and restriction) **but** in this case **these are sweetened *dinim*** judgments **and are** referred to in the verse (Judges 9:13) as '**the wine that gladdens.' It is inappropriate to** even **call these types of *gevurot* 'judgments' for that term implies judging each according to his deeds to chastise and punish, Heaven forfend. Indeed, the opposite is true – sweetened *dinim* benefit the world, and in this way He is good and does good for the wicked and for the righteous.**

**This is also the concept of the Thirteen Attributes** (*Middot*) **of Divine Mercy, which are also *gevurot*, which is why they are called *middot*, for they disburse compassion in a measured manner, not broadly. Nevertheless, they subdue all the harsh judgments because they are sweetened *gevurot*, as the Rabbi** the Arizal **writes regarding all this in** *Pri Eitz Chaim **Sha'ar haShofar*** **chaps. 1 & 2\. See there.**

**All of the 13 Attributes of Divine Mercy are found and revealed in *binah*, which is** nourished by **the last *mazal*** – *mazal* in the sense of 'constant outflow**'** from *nozel*, flowing, not in the sense of 'constellations' – this is the *mazal* of **ונקה "and He cleanses"** (Exodus 34:7). **This is the 13th Tikun** of the 13 *Tikunei Dikna* (see footnote 6\) **which includes all the others. Thus *binah* is in** the category of **the inner chambers, as explained above, and therefore it is certainly so and obvious that there "might and joy** **are in His place" and judgments are not actually revealed other than below, in the outer chambers, for that is where sin and blemish reach and compassion is concealed, so that the *gevurot* become actual *dinim*** – harsh judgments – **thereby.**

**Thus there is no claim at all from the fact that whatever is below is only from above, for although this is certainly the case, nevertheless the *dinim* only become revealed in a place where sin and blemish are of great consequence since compassion is concealed. This is only in the outer chambers, whereas the inner chambers are always** **aromatic**.

**So it turns out that even though that whatever is below is only from above, the revelation of *dinim* as actual *dinim*** i.e. harsh judgments – **is always in the world below exclusively. Furthermore, when suffering grows** down below, **then either through prayer or through crying out** in pain, **compassion is aroused above, subduing the *dinim* below due to the revelations from above. This means to say that the *gevurot* above are revealed down below, but because those *gevurot* are always fragrant, the *gevurot* below also become fragrant.**

**In any event, above, in the inner chambers, they** the *dinim* **are always fragrant, so that there "might and joy** **are in His place," except at the of the destruction, may the Merciful One spare us, for then the order of Creation was upended completely and weeping and the revelation of *dinim*** in the negative sense **and weeping impacted on high as well, i.e. to the place of *mistarim* above, as was all explained earlier. Accordingly, the words of the Ramak are very surprising in my view.**

**In addition the proofs he** the Ramak **cites from several statements of the Zohar are also surprising in my view. For what the *Zohar, Noach* 61b states regarding the destruction** of the Beit HaMikdash: **'And thus there is certainly sadness in His presence in the outer chambers, and this is the meaning of the verse, "Behold, the valiant ones weep outside…" and "the Lord, G-d of legions, called it** a day for weeping and mourning…"' **– the language** of these verses **expresses the opposite: that even at the time of destruction weeping took place only in the outer chambers\! This is the meaning of:  'And thus there is certainly sadness in His presence in the outer chambers' – where the expression 'certainly' implies that it is as if it said that the possibility for sadness is only there** in the outer chambers, **and not in the inner chambers at all. Whereas the Ramak explains that the Zohar's intention is that during the destruction sadness was manifested as a new phenomenon even in the outer chambers, like the Talmud states according to Rashi's version.**

**But the language of the Zohar does not indicate this at all, but rather that even at the time of the destruction there was weeping *only* in the outer chambers, meaning to say, the outer chambers of Atzilut, which is in *ze'ir anpin* and *malchut*, and there sadness is also manifested, as the *Idra Rabba* 144a explains on the verse** (Genesis 6:6) **"He was saddened to His heart," which is the inner chambers of the first aspect we mentioned above. This is because compared with the Worlds of BiY"A, *ze'ir anpin* and *malchut* of Atzilut are called 'inner chambers,' as the Zohar *Shemot*, 18a states. But compared with Abba and Imma, *ze'ir anpin* and *malchut* are the only the outer chambers, as explained above.**

**The Zohar in *parshat Noach* on our subject** cited by the Ramak **is that even at the time of destruction there was weeping only in *ze'ir anpin* and *malchut*, but not in Abba and Imma. This does not comport with the view of the Talmud in C*hagiga* 5b as explained above, since at the time of the destruction there was weeping even in Abba and Imma, in the place of *mistarim*, as we explained above.**

**However, the primary view seems to be as later authorities write, that this entire section of the Zohar, which is in parentheses from the words האי אמריתו to וגומר is not part of the Zohar at all, but an addition from some student, and so it seems from the language as well; it is not the language of the Zohar at all. Accordingly the primary view is that of the Talmud – that at the time of the destruction there was weeping even in the inner chambers above, in both aspects of *mistarim*, which is why *mistarim* is stated in the plural, as we explained. So there was weeping in the place of *mistarim* above as well. And this is understood explicitly from the Zohar *Shemot* 18a, that at that time there was weeping in the inner chambers above, but only in the *mistarim* there, as it states explicitly** **there 17b *– b'mistarim*.** **See there.**

**From all of this it seems that the entire addition in the note in** Zohar **Noach 61b, which states that 'Thus there is certainly sadness in His presence in the outer chambers,' is of the view that even at the time of the destruction there was weeping in *ze'ir anpin* and *malchut* exclusively. But this is not the primary view. Accordingly, whether** one explains it **this way or that way it cannot be a proof for the Ramak's view. To the contrary, it stands in contradiction to his words. Understand all of this well and you will see that this is indeed so, since 'truth is its own witness.'**[^10]

**The Ramak brings another proof for his view that the primary potency of *dinim* and where weeping is appropriate is only in *binah,* which is of the inner chambers. He cites the Zohar *Noach*, 70b, which states that when supernal fire, which is *binah*, acts to increase the *dinim* down below in *malchut*, then supernal ire becomes even more potent and destroys everything, since it does not cease. Meaning to say that the ire does not abate until the judgment is carried out in full, and this is what transpired during the flood, which is why the entire world was destroyed, and only Noah and his family remained. And even he had to hide away in the Ark, invisible to the eye, as it states there p. 64b. A similar exposition is proffered in** Zohar ***Pikudei*** **227b. But when strict judgment** *din* **is drawn down from *malchut* alone, without its strength being increased by *binah*, then the *din* is made to be halted, and it does not cause total destruction, Heaven forfend. See there.**

**The Ramak brings a proof from the above that the primary potency of *dinim* is in *binah*, which is from the inner chambers, and therefore the primary quality of sadness and weeping is relevant there exclusively.**

**Now although a superficial view of the matters mentioned there would seem to imply that this is indeed so, anyone who is somewhat familiar with the words of the holy Zohar in other places, and particularly the Ramak, for whom the gates of the Zohar were wide open, it is surprising that a holy mouth like his could utter such words\! For it is known that *binah* itself is the supernal reservoir from which all blessings emerge (as in** Zohar ***Noach*, 64b; *Terumah* 162a; *Acharei,* 65a; *Va'etchanan* 264b. And examine *Pri Eitz Chaim, Sha'ar haBerachot* chap. 1). For she** *binah* **is** all **compassion, albeit that from her side** i.e. the left column, which she heads, ***dinim* can be aroused as is written in** Zohar ***Vayikra*** **10b and *Acharei* 65a.**

**But *malchut* is *din* as in** Zohar ***Noach*, 64b and *Vayikra* 11b, regarding which** the Sages stated[^11] **"*dina d'malchuta dina"*** which means literally that the law of the state is the law. However *din* also means harsh judgment, as we have been explaining all along, and *malchuta* can mean the state but also alludes to *malchut*. **Now all of this is straightforward and is known from the words of all the Kabbalists, and it is true that *binah* is the root** specifically **of the *gevurot* and she is the 'dark lamp' and the supernal fire mentioned in *parshat Noach* above.**

**Nevertheless, she** *binah* **is called only 'pure fire' as explained in the *Idra Rabba* p. 135b because all of the *gevurot* within her are all sweetened and fragrant, as mentioned above. As for** what the Zohar states **in *parshat Noach,* that sometimes potent *dinim* are also drawn down from her, the Zohar explains in *parshat Acharei* p. 65a that this is only when wicked people change compassion into harsh judgment. Then certainly *din* is empowered greatly, since the sins** of those wicked people **caused compassion to be transformed into harsh judgments, and those harsh judgments have an impact all the way up to *binah*.**

**Now it is well known that the loftier the *sefirah* the more superior its qualities are compared with the lower *sefirot*, and incomparably so, as the Rav** the Arizal **writes in** *Eitz Chaim **Mochin d'Tzelem*** **chap 2 and in *Derushei Abiy'a* chap. 14\. Therefore, when a sin has the effect of revealing *dinim* even in *binah,* due to the wicked transforming compassion into harsh judgment, then certainly *dinim* become very powerful. And this is what the Zohar said in *parshat Noach*** 70b **– that as long as the supernal fire continues to strengthen, then smoke, which is *din* down below, strengthens the ire and destroys everything. This is because the ire does not abate until judgment is carried out in full. But when *din* down below does not continue to grow more powerful together with the *din*  above, then a judgment is made and the *din* abates and does not destroy completely. And for this reason the verse** (Genesis 8:21) **states, "I will not continue to curse the land because of man."** In other words, **I will not continue to give additional strength to *din* down below. The quote from the Zohar ends here.**

**All of this is because the *gevurot*** of *binah* **are without question** **much greater than the *gevurot* of *malchut,* comparable to the superiority of *binah* over *malchut*, which is immeasurable. But** the *gevurot* of *binah* **are all fragrant and sweetened, and therefore the entire reward of the World-to-Come and all the concealed goodness is in *binah,* for she is the** **World-to-Come, as is known.**

**But when sin and depravity have the effect of revealing *dinim* even in *binah,* because compassion was transformed into harsh judgment and in place of the flames of compassion complete *din* is produced, may the Merciful One spare us, then certainly the *dinim* become very, very powerful, to the point of destruction, Heaven forfend, just as at the time of the Flood.**

**Now in truth, the blessed Holy One already made a promise that "I will not continue to curse the earth because of man," and He made a covenant in this regard, the Covenant of the Rainbow. Thus *binah* always remains in her praiseworthy loftiness, which are the flames of compassion, and in her are hidden and concealed all the good of the future and the rewards of the World-to-Come. And this is in the inner chambers above, as we said, regarding which it states, "Might and joy are in His place." Only in the place of *mistarim* therein is there sadness and weeping for the destruction of the Beit HaMikdash, as explained above.**

**Thus it has been explained that the citations from the Zohar are not a proof at all that there is weeping in the inner chambers** (other than with the destruction of the Beit HaMikdash), **and the words of the Ramak remain very surprising in my view.** **For the Zohar *Vayikra* 15b states explicitly** **that sadness is *only*** **in the outer chambers, and not in the inner chambers, Heaven forfend – meaning the outer chambers of Atzilut, as explained above. It is only up to there that sadness reaches and no further, Heaven forfend. And this is also what the *Idra Rabba* 144a states on the verse** (Genesis 6:6) **"He was saddened to His heart," see there.**

**Now this is just like the original and older version of the Talmud explicated by Rabbeinu Chananel, which is the primary version, as the Radvaz and Rabbi Todros in *Otzer HaKavod* state, as noted earlier.** As regards **the effort the Ramak made to establish Rashi's version, one who examines his words will see that he did not know that the primary version states the opposite, and he surmised that only Rabbi Todros HaLevi and the *Sha'arei Tzedek* found a different version of the Gemara. See *Pardes, Sha'ar* 8:12. And therefore he toiled to establish Rashi's version, which was the original version in his view. But since we have merited the words of Rabbeinu Chananel, and the testimony of the Radvaz that this is the version in the older works, it has become clear that the original version of the Gemara is also found in the words of the Zohar and the Kabbalists. This being so, we need no longer be in doubt about this, and the truth will show the way and will be called the holy way.**
